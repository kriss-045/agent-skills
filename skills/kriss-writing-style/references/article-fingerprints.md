# Article Fingerprints

One line per source article: what that specific piece does differently. This preserves the per-article ethos, the point was never a single averaged voice, but a writer who picks different moves for different pieces. Scan this to remember the range. Full forensic analyses are archived outside the skill (in Kriss's build folder) if deeper detail is ever needed.

Eras: **2020** = raw, ESL-inflected, dash-heavy origin voice. **2022 to 2023** = mature voice, same DNA, polished. Write in the mature register (see carry-forward table in `voice-tone-and-rhythm.md`).

## 2022 to 2023 (mature voice)
- **12 mistakes junior designers need to avoid**, peer-mentor listicle; earns authority by admitting each tip is obvious, then defends it with a real anecdote. Best example of the anecdote-listicle + disarmer.
- **Feature Design Checklist 2.0**, warm, self-deprecating intro then a near-pure numbered checklist; voice survives in parenthetical real-world examples and direct "you" questions. Shows restraint.
- **Design of Everyday Things in 16 visuals**, "I forget everything I read" everyman reframes someone else's classic book through one running worked-example (the humble input box). Analogy-first teaching at its purest.
- **4 things to do to be luckier in your career**, borrowed idea (a podcast/research) made personal by stacking confessional autobiography on each principle, then handing the reader the same homework.
- **9 Lessons for first-time Leaders**, listicle with a running Elon-Musk anti-example as a through-line; home of the abandoned-metaphor bit ("wait… this is not working. Damn it.").
- **How to write better case studies**, hiring-manager confessional; voices the reader's rationalizations ("And do you think that by not showing sketches... I will think you are not a UX designer? No.") then knocks them down.

## 2020 (raw origin voice)
- **What USA and UK need to learn from Dubai**, earnest op-ed on rhetorical-question headers; conviction outruns evidence; a single (misattributed) quote does the closing.
- **How to create better education system**, policy opinion as a chain of self-posed questions answered plainly; idealistic civic voice; "Let a kid be a kid."
- **Why you are already winning at life**, cold-open on real workplace dialogue (Bob, Hussain, Will); lets co-workers deliver the heavy lines; turns their resignation into reassurance for the reader.
- **Types of Career**, profane barstool taxonomy of six career types; each a character study with a verdict baked in; rawest, most cynical register in the corpus.
- **Why it is wrong to plan your dream life**, bait-and-switch open (fake flex → "Okay, that was a straight up lie"); the form is the argument; college-degree analogy for building a passion career.
- **How not to get scammed by online gurus**, FAQ/Socratic question-ladder; performs the scam move then revokes it to earn trust; ends on the shortest hardest line ("Just run").
- **Why most people don't care about your story**, single-insight reframe ("be different, not unique"); borrowed quote restated flat; closes on the marathon-not-sprint image.
- **A bad day is a better future**, mood-arc pep talk built on physics metaphors (sine wave, rolling ball, fire/fuel); the embryo of his analogy-first instinct.
- **The question you need to ask yourself**, named-character parable (Sham vs. Bob); ring structure (opening question = closing CTA to comments).
- **How to win like Red Bull**, stat-shock case breakdown ($0.09 vs $3.49 as a ledger); History → Strategy → Lessons; collapses to one keyword ("unique") at the end.
- **Why society is not promoting creativity**, first-principles musing built on anaphoric triads and reader-implicating "you and me"; the circular-object thought experiment runs the thesis inside your head.
- **Apple Has Done It Again**, bait-and-switch essay; two-thirds admiration, then reframes minimalism as cynical marketing; triple "If Apple really..." anaphora as a prosecutorial climax; "I will leave that blank space for you to fill up."
