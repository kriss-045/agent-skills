# Craft principles + red-flag audit

The rules that separate a case study that reads as senior from one that reads as junior, each with the reason and the source. Enforce these while drafting (Step 4) and check them in the self-audit (Step 7).

<a name="reader"></a>
## 1. Who reads this, and how

The reader is a screener, not a fan. The measured behavior (Fountain Institute hiring research, corroborated across the dissections): a portfolio review lasts 2 to 3 minutes, the homepage gets 10 to 15 seconds, and a large majority of recruiters run an AI-assisted screen before a human opens it. Two consequences: the piece must be skimmable and front-loaded, and it must read as senior in the parts a skim lands on (openings, section names, the one hard part).

<a name="principles"></a>
## 2. The enforced principles

1. **Role early and specific.** State what Kriss did, in the first sentence or first section, honestly about team size but with a specific personal claim ("I pushed for the research", "built it from zero"). Reason: every dissected exemplar did this; a reader who cannot tell what you did in 10 seconds bounces. Source: all exemplars (Pan, Fu, Kosas, Saarinen).

2. **No process-phase spine (default).** Do not use Discover / Define / Ideate / Deliver. Name sections after the decision or the problem. Reason: not one senior exemplar used a process spine; it reads as coursework. Only the explicitly junior-tier types (6, 7) may use it. Source: portfolio-case-study-structures.md, target-companies.md.

3. **Sections sized by decision weight.** The hardest decision gets the most words and is signposted as such ("the biggest pain point"). Reason: it shows judgment about what mattered, which is the senior read. Source: Kosas, Caston dissections.

4. **Evidence inside the decision it justifies.** A user quote or number sits next to the screen it changed, never collected into a standalone research chapter. Reason: a research chapter reads as process theatre; evidence-in-place reads as reasoning. Source: all three conventional exemplars.

5. **Numbers rare and specific.** One or a few hard, specific numbers, never a KPI wall, and never a fabricated one. Reason: specificity reads as real; a wall reads as padding; a fabricated number is a fire-on-sight error for a screener. Source: exemplars (one metric each); resume-research folklore section on invented claims.

6. **Trade-offs and one honest hard part are the senior signal.** Show what was tried first, what did not work, what was traded away and kept. Include a "what was hard / what I'd change" beat. Reason: "seniority reads through trade-offs, not polish" and the honest-hard-part section was repeatedly the one that read as senior. Source: Fountain Institute; Caston (ends on Challenges); Pan (killed an idea).

7. **Front-load and keep it skimmable.** Hook or mandate or metrics up top; short sections; the point of each section legible from its heading. Reason: the 2-to-3-minute read and the AI pre-screen. Source: hiring research.

8. **Professional register throughout, Kriss's cadence at the sentence layer.** Draft as a hiring document from the first line; take from kriss-writing-style only the sentence-level voice (contractions, spoken openers, plain words, hard-varied sentence lengths, his verbatim phrasings from the interview). No metaphor families, no everyday analogies or examples, no jokes, no hand-off closes. Reason: Kriss rejected a draft for a sustained "closing table" metaphor ("this is a professional case study, it should not have example of closing table"). Source: Kriss feedback, 2026-08-10 (supersedes the 2026-08-09 voice-then-register order).

9. **Narrative pull, no context dump.** Open on the tension, not on a "what is X" explainer; introduce domain rules inside the section where the reader needs them; end sections by raising the next problem. Section titles state what happened, specific over thematic. Reason: Kriss rejected a draft for a front-loaded context section, "basic" section titles, and flow that "doesn't keep me hooked." Source: Kriss feedback, 2026-08-10.

10. **Zero AI tells.** Hunt and kill: not-X-but-Y symmetry, evenly sized sentences, formal words where plain ones exist, clever compressed constructions, punchline endings on every paragraph. Kriss's cadence is long additive spoken sentences with occasional short ones. Reason: Kriss sent a draft back solely for AI-flavored sentence structure ("zero AI hints from it"). Source: Kriss feedback, 2026-08-10.

<a name="audit"></a>
## 3. Red-flag self-audit

Check every flag; fix each hit before presenting. Report the result in one line.

- [ ] **Process-spine creep** — any Discover/Define/Ideate/Deliver (or disguised equivalent) in a non-junior type? Rename to decision/problem sections.
- [ ] **Buried role** — can the reader NOT tell what Kriss did in the first 10 seconds? Move the role up and make it specific.
- [ ] **Research chapter** — is evidence collected into a standalone research/persona/competitive section instead of sitting in the decision it justifies? Redistribute it.
- [ ] **KPI wall or fabricated number** — more than a few metrics, or any number Kriss did not actually provide? Cut to the few real ones; replace any invented figure with a qualitative outcome and flag it.
- [ ] **No trade-off, no honest hard part** — is the piece all triumph? Add the "what was hard / what I'd change" beat.
- [ ] **Wrong length for the type** — does the draft blow past or fall short of the chosen type's target without a reason? Retarget to the taxonomy budget.
- [ ] **Flat, unskimmable open** — does it start with setup/scene-padding instead of a hook, mandate, or metric? Front-load it.
- [ ] **Generic portfolio-speak** — did the voice pass actually run, or does it read like anyone's template? Re-run kriss-writing-style, then the register lift.
- [ ] **Unconfirmed confidential detail** — any employer specifics not cleared as publicly showable? Mark internal-only and list what needs Kriss's clearance.
- [ ] **Metaphor / analogy creep** — any sustained metaphor, everyday-life analogy, or casual example in the draft? Cut it entirely; explain the domain directly. (Kriss, 2026-08-10.)
- [ ] **Context dump** — does a "what is X" explainer section precede the story? Fold the domain rules into the sections that need them and open on the tension.
- [ ] **AI-flavored sentences** — not-X-but-Y symmetry, uniform sentence lengths, formal words, punchline paragraph endings? Rewrite at the sentence layer in Kriss's spoken cadence.
